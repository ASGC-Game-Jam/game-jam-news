Server report: Jun 21 – Jun 28, 2026

Leads assemble: polls go live, Unreal takes the lead, and the ASGC Game Jam gets its scaffolding

1509 messages · 49 active members · 21 active channels

This was the week the ASGC Game Jam leadership came together. On Jun 22 GreenCat welcomed Wave One, the Discipline Leads, with Diana Donado as Creative Director and Sidds (shadowarrior) as Executive Producer. Xelnath (Alex) set naming conventions and succession rules: a departing lead nominates a replacement, and the leads vote by majority. He also kicked off the Day 0 task, where each discipline submitted one 5-option poll question so the community could pick the jam's 'seed'. His guidance was to scope for 6 weeks, not 6 months, and to embrace 'uncomfortable constraints'. Polls came in from Narrative, Design, Art, Audio, QA, Tech and Production. Early frontrunners were Steampunk, Detective Thriller Mystery and Exploring New Worlds, with Mystery leading by about 50%. Amir's LinkedIn poll results were due to be announced. On Fri Jun 26 the second wave arrived, renamed from 'Feature Leads' to 'Deputy Leads'. The structure is now 16 discipline leads, each with 2–4 deputies, supporting a 300+ person jam. About 20 leads introduced themselves, with backgrounds spanning Epic, Rocket League, For Honor, Final Fantasy VII QA, Gameloft and Roblox.

Behind the scenes, the production team built the project's operating system. Sidds created the Gameplan Scratchpad, Master Questionnaire, Leads Contact Info sheet and a blocker-resolution flowchart, and ran the first Leads Sync on Jun 24. Michal Fried collected all of these in #lead-only-resources. Production picked ClickUp for the schedule, daily Miro blocker checks, a weekly sync and an onboarding checklist. Lori drafted a 26-week milestone plan (Alpha 6, Beta 6, RC 6, GM 5, Final 3). VibeBlind proposed a weekly cycle ending in a stable Friday build. Tool debates ran across many channels: GitHub Issues vs. Notion vs. ClickUp vs. Miro. QA and Tech settled on GitHub Issues with bug templates and the Triage access level.

Technical direction firmed up. VibeBlind said the team will likely use Unreal, pending the Art Lead's confirmation and the poll results, and most leads backed it for industry relevance. Some raised concerns about hardware, disk space and Paper2D. Adrian flagged GitHub's free-tier storage and LFS bandwidth limits. Xelnath stressed that the source must stay publicly served, and the question is still open. Each discipline also posted its AI/LLM stance. The broad consensus: AI is fine for tools, debugging and support work with disclosure and human review, but largely not for final content. Art locked in a 'no AI final artwork' policy. Design and Tech also aligned on a GDD (why) / TDD (how) / GitHub Issue (what) documentation split.
