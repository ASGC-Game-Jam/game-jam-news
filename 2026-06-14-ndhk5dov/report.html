Jam prep kicks off: Xelnath hands leads their Day 0 checklist and puts AI rules in each discipline's hands

16 messages · 2 active members · 2 active channels

This week was all about laying the groundwork for the upcoming game jam, and nearly all of it came from Xelnath on Friday, June 12. In #Day-0-Tasks for Leads, he posted a seven-item checklist for discipline leads. Each discipline (Narrative, Design, Art, Programming, Production and others) is asked to propose 5 options for a community poll that Amir will run. The top results will seed the jam: a Narrative theme, a Design genre, an Art style, the main character's animal species from Programming, and one special limitation from Production.

The checklist also asks leads to sort out how their team works internally: how decisions get made, how assets are committed, how people notify each other, and how rework and feedback are handled. Leads should also nail down the GitHub workflow (adding people, working on the project, merging), plan documentation, prepare onboarding material for their discipline, request the roles, powers and channels they need, and watch the leads onboarding video. For docs, Xelnath recommended a master Google Doc index that links to short 1–2 page detail docs.

Over in #Day-1-LLM Based Generation & Tools, Xelnath opened the question of AI and LLM use during the event. The ruling is that each discipline decides for itself but not for others, and the mods won't make the call. Disciplines should discuss content generation, tool generation and debugging/refinement separately and reach a decision by the end of Day 2 so the rules can go out to participants. No leads had replied in either channel by the end of the week, so the ball is now in their court.

Themes

  • Discipline leads own the decisions — Both channels push decision-making down to each discipline. Leads choose their poll options and internal processes, and each discipline sets its own AI-usage policy without overruling the others. (#Day-0-Tasks for Leads, #Day-1-LLM Based Generation & Tools)
  • Community poll to seed the jam — Each discipline proposes 5 options for Amir's poll: theme, genre, art style, main character animal species and a special limitation. The winning picks become the jam's starting brief. (#Day-0-Tasks for Leads)
  • AI/LLM usage rules by category — Disciplines are asked to rule separately on content generation, tool generation and debugging/refinement. For example, art can ban AI for art while programming allows it for tools. Decisions are due by the end of Day 2. (#Day-1-LLM Based Generation & Tools)
  • Workflow, docs and onboarding — Leads need to define GitHub practices, asset commits, feedback loops and onboarding material. Documentation should follow a master Google Doc index linking to short detail docs. (#Day-0-Tasks for Leads)

#Day-1-LLM Based Generation & Tools

4 messages from 1 people

On Friday, June 12, Xelnath kicked off a discussion in #Day-1-LLM Based Generation & Tools about how LLM/AI generation may be used during the event. He laid out the current ruling: each discipline decides for itself, but not for the others. If the art team doesn't want it, it's banned for art use; if programming decides it's fine for tools, it's permitted; and QA can choose whether to automate with it.

Xelnath said the mods will not make the call themselves. Instead, he recommended each discipline discuss three categories separately: (1) content generation, (2) tool generation, and (3) debugging/refinement. Each discipline should reach a decision by the end of Day 2 so the rules can be distributed to participants. No discipline had responded or made a decision yet within this window.
